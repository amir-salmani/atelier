# Intake

The gathering half of [discipline 0](disciplines.md). Its output is the
[evidence pool](evidence.md) and a thesis that can be proven wrong.

## The one rule

**Ask about the past, never about the future.**

*"Would you use a tool that did X?"* produces politeness. *"Tell me about the
last time you had to do X"* produces a story with a date, a frequency and a
workaround in it — which is an evidence item.

People are unreliable about what they will do and very reliable about what they
did. Every question below is a way of asking the second thing.

## The five questions of discipline 0, as interview

| Discipline 0 asks | You ask |
|---|---|
| Who, specifically | *"Walk me through yesterday."* Not *"describe your role."* |
| What breaks today | *"When did this last go wrong? What did you do?"* |
| What they do instead | *"Show me how you do it now."* Then watch — the spreadsheet is the competitor |
| Where the money is | *"What does an hour of this cost you? What do you pay for today?"* |
| Why now | *"What changed this year?"* If nothing did, the window is a guess |

## What to do while they talk

- **Shut up.** The silence after an answer is where the second half arrives.
- **Chase the workaround.** A spreadsheet, a WhatsApp group, a notebook, a
  person they phone. Every workaround is a feature someone already built by
  hand, and it is the highest-value thing in the conversation.
- **Ask for the number**, badly if necessary. *"Roughly — once a week? Once a
  day?"* An approximate frequency beats none, and they will correct you.
- **Do not demo.** The moment you show something, you stop collecting and start
  selling, and everything after is contaminated.
- **Do not take the feature request.** Take the thing behind it. *"What would
  you do with the export?"*

## Straight after, not later

Write the items into the pool **the same day**, one per thing that happened,
with the date and the source named. A conversation written up a week later
becomes a summary of your impressions, and impressions are what the pool exists
to keep out.

Mark confidence honestly: something they described in detail with a date is
`confirmed`; something they mentioned in passing is `reported`; something you
inferred is `unverified` and needs corroboration before a journey leans on it.

## How much is enough

**Stop when you stop being surprised.** Usually five to eight conversations,
often fewer if you are also operating the thing yourself. If the sixth
conversation produces three new items, you are not finished.

That is a stopping rule, not a target. Twenty interviews producing a hundred
items is not more rigour — it is a pool nobody will read, and
[evidence-lint](evidence.md) will show most of it unused.

## The output

Two artifacts, and discipline 0 does not close without both:

1. **The pool** — [evidence.md](evidence.md), linted.
2. **A one-page thesis with a falsifiable claim and a kill condition.** The
   claim names a person, a behaviour and a number. The kill condition is what
   would make you stop, stated before you are emotionally invested in not
   stopping.

## What this is not

Not market sizing, not a competitor matrix, not a survey. Those answer *how big*
and this answers *is it real*. **A product can be wrong about the first and
survive; wrong about the second and nothing downstream matters** — which is why
discipline 0 exists at all.

# inspire — choosing a direction

**Read `../../method/inspire.md`** for the procedure, then
**`../../method/originality.md`** for the gate every direction has to pass.
Both are short, and both sit two directories above this file.

`teardown` writes facts. This makes choices. Do not merge them.

## The shape

**Three directions. One of them is the quiet one** — no new mechanism, existing
components, the argument won on restraint. Mandatory: three novel directions is
a rigged ballot, and the quiet one wins more often than expected.

Each direction commits to **three numbers, not adjectives**: tempo (median ms),
easing (one named curve), stagger (one constant in ms). Plus type, colour and
accent policy, exactly one structural idea, the reduced-motion behaviour, the
cost, a provenance table, the inversion, and a kill condition.

## The gate

Mechanism may be taken. **Dressing never.** Every element traces to **two or
more unrelated teardowns, or one constraint of this product** — a single source
is a lift. Rows that failed stay in the provenance table; a table with no
rejections was not audited.

Then the swap test, the inversion, and the subtraction pass twice.

## Order of work

1. **Journeys first, library second.** Opening the teardowns first produces a
   direction about the teardowns.
2. The project's design system, for the rules a direction must survive.
3. `grep` the library for the mechanism needed — do not re-read it all.
4. Three directions, gated, then recommend one and say what it costs.
5. It becomes a decision record with a date.

## Do not

- **Do not produce a mood board.** Images without numbers cannot be built,
  reviewed or falsified.
- **Do not propose a direction sourced from one site.**
- **Do not drop the quiet direction** because the others are more exciting.
- **Do not invent tokens.** Name what the design system will need to add.
- **Do not describe motion from a screenshot** — that is `teardown`'s job, and
  it requires actually running it.
